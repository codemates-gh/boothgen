Every photo booth operator who's been in business for more than a year has a story. A cancellation with no notice. A client who disputed the deposit. An event where expectations hadn't been set clearly and the conversation got uncomfortable. In almost every case, the root cause was the same: no signed contract, or a contract that wasn't clear enough.

Contracts aren't just legal protection — they set expectations, establish professionalism, and give both parties a reference point when things get complicated.

## The Problem With Word Doc Contracts

The traditional workflow looks like this: customize a template in Word, save as PDF, email it, wait for the client to print, sign, scan, and email back. Some clients do it quickly. Most take days. Some never do it at all — and you follow up three times before giving up.

The back-and-forth is annoying. The tracking is manual. And a scan of a handwritten signature on a printed PDF is a weaker legal instrument than a properly timestamped digital signature.

## What Makes a Digital Contract Flow Work

A good digital contract workflow has three properties:

**1. It's frictionless for the client.** They click a link, read the contract, type their name, click sign. Done. No printing, no scanning, no separate app or account.

**2. It's connected to the booking.** The signed contract should live in the same place as the quote, the invoice, and the photos — not in a separate inbox folder or a DocuSign account the client can't easily access again.

**3. It creates a clear record.** Timestamp, IP address, signer name — all captured automatically, available to both parties at any time.

## Both Parties Sign

Booth Genius supports dual signatures — client and operator. You can sign from your dashboard before or after the client signs. The contract isn't considered complete until both signatures are captured.

This matters legally in most jurisdictions. A contract signed by only one party can be challenged. A timestamped, dual-signature document is much harder to dispute.

## What to Include in Your Photo Booth Contract

Your specific legal terms should come from an attorney familiar with event services in your jurisdiction. That said, most photo booth contracts cover:

- **Service description** — what equipment, how many hours, what's included
- **Payment schedule** — deposit amount, due date, final balance due date
- **Cancellation policy** — what happens if the client cancels within 30/60/90 days
- **Rescheduling policy** — how date changes are handled
- **Venue requirements** — power needs, space requirements, floor type restrictions
- **Liability limitations** — what you're responsible for and what you're not
- **Force majeure** — events beyond your control (weather, illness, equipment failure)

A template you configure once, with merge tags for client-specific details, covers all of this without rewriting anything for each event.

## The Confidence to Enforce Your Policies

Operators without clear contracts often find themselves eating costs they shouldn't have to absorb — a last-minute cancellation, a venue that had incompatible power, a client who expected things that were never discussed. With a signed contract on file, the conversation changes. You're not the one making a claim — you're both referencing an agreement you both signed.

That confidence alone is worth the 20 minutes it takes to set up your contract template.

The fastest setups don't happen at the venue. They happen the night before, at your desk, with a checklist.

Here's the one we'd use. Copy it, change it to fit your gear, and keep it in your case.

## The day before: at your desk

Most setup problems come from decisions left for the venue. Make them now.

- **Confirm the details with your client.** Start time, end time, the exact spot for the booth, the nearest outlet, and who your contact is on the day.
- **Build the event.** Layout, colors, logo or names, countdown, number of photos per session, sharing options. If your booth app has event defaults, start from those and change only what's different.
- **Check the layout on a real photo.** Names spelled right? Date right? Logo sharp? This is the most common last-minute fix, and it's easy to catch now.
- **Decide on sharing.** Text, email, QR code or all three. Write a short message guests will see with their photos.
- **Pay for the event or add it to the invoice**, so nothing on the booth screen says "unpaid" in front of guests.

With Genie, all of this happens at boothgen.com from your laptop. At the venue, the iPad picks up the event and you're ready.

## The day before: your gear

- iPad charged, with the booth app updated
- Printer, paper and ink: enough for the expected crowd, plus a spare pack
- The print box or laptop that runs your printer
- Stand, lights, backdrop and clamps
- Props, if the event has them
- Power strip and an extension cord (venue outlets are never where you need them)
- Gaffer tape for cables
- A phone hotspot as backup internet

Pack in the same order every time. If something has a home in your case, you notice when it's missing.

## At the venue: the 20 minutes

**Minutes 0 to 5: place and power.** Put the booth where your contact asked, with a clear spot for a line to form. Run power and tape down every cable people could trip over.

**Minutes 5 to 12: build.** Stand, iPad, lights, backdrop. Point the lights so faces are evenly lit, without glare on glasses.

**Minutes 12 to 17: connect and check.**

- Open the event on the iPad. If it's the first time on this iPad, pair it with a pairing code.
- Set the attendant PIN so guests can't leave the booth screen.
- Connect to the venue Wi-Fi, or your hotspot if the Wi-Fi is bad.
- Turn on the printer and print a test.

**Minutes 17 to 20: test session.** Do one full session yourself, as a guest would: tap to start, pose, review, share to your own phone, print. If the photo arrives on your phone and the print looks right, you're ready.

## During the event

- Check the paper level every hour or so.
- Watch the line. If it's long, turn down the photos per session or the review time so it moves faster.
- If the Wi-Fi drops, keep going. A good booth app saves photos and sends them when it's back online.

## After the event

- Make sure every photo has uploaded before you pack up the iPad.
- Send your client the gallery link while the night is still fresh.
- Restock paper and charge everything as soon as you're home, so the next event starts ready.

## Why it matters

A calm, quick setup isn't just less stress. Clients notice when the booth is ready before guests arrive, and they mention it in reviews. The best part: almost all of the work happens the night before, where it's easy.

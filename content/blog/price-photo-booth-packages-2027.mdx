Most photo booth operators set their prices once, early on, by looking at what the competitor down the road charges. Then they leave them alone for years while their costs, their skills and their reviews all go up.

If you're planning 2027, now is the time to fix that. Here's a practical way to think about it.

## Start with what an event really costs you

Before you look at anyone else's prices, work out your own floor. For a typical 3-hour event, add up:

- **Your time, door to door.** Loading, driving, setup, the event itself, teardown, unloading. A 3-hour booking is often 6 to 7 hours of work.
- **Staff**, if you send an attendant.
- **Prints and media.** Paper and ink per print, multiplied by a realistic number of sessions.
- **Software and sharing.** Your booth app, texting, gallery hosting, your CRM.
- **Wear and replacement.** iPads, printers, lights and backdrops don't last forever. Set a little aside from every event.
- **Travel.** Fuel, tolls, parking.

Whatever that total is, it's your break-even, not your price. Your price needs room for profit, slow months and growth.

## Hourly or packages?

**Hourly pricing** is easy to explain, but it invites comparison shopping. A client sees "$150/hour" next to "$125/hour" and stops reading.

**Packages** move the conversation from "how cheap" to "which one". Three tiers work well for most operators:

1. **Essentials.** The booth, digital sharing, a standard layout. It sets your entry point.
2. **Signature.** Unlimited prints, a custom-designed layout, props and an online gallery. This is the one you want most clients to pick, so make it the obvious value.
3. **Premium.** Everything in Signature, plus a longer booking, a custom backdrop, boomerangs and an attendant for the whole event.

When the middle package is clearly the best deal, most clients choose it, and your average booking goes up without any hard selling.

## Add-ons worth offering

Add-ons let clients make the event theirs, and they raise your average order. Good ones cost you little and look great in photos:

- **Custom layout design** with the couple's names, the company logo or the event colors
- **Extra hours**, priced a little below your hourly rate so they're an easy yes
- **Premium backdrops** or virtual backdrops
- **Props**, physical or digital
- **A guest book**: a print of every session for the hosts
- **Rush design** for bookings inside two weeks

Keep the list short. Five or six well-chosen add-ons sell better than twenty.

## Raising your prices for 2027

If you're booked solid on Saturdays in peak season, your prices are too low. Some ways to raise them without losing your best clients:

- **Raise prices for new bookings only.** Honor every quote and contract already sent.
- **Give notice.** "Our 2027 prices take effect January 1. Book before then to lock in this year's rate." It's honest, and it gets fence-sitters to sign.
- **Add value at the same time.** A better layout, faster delivery or a new backdrop makes the new price easy to justify.
- **Raise the middle package most.** It's the one most clients choose, so it has the biggest effect on revenue.

## The short version

- Know your real cost per event before you set a price.
- Use three packages and make the middle one the obvious choice.
- Offer a handful of add-ons that cost you little.
- Raise prices for new bookings, with notice, and add value when you do.

Your prices should reflect the business you're building in 2027, not the one you started with.
